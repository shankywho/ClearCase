# Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006
## Chapter IV & V: Boundaries, Boundary Marks and Demarcation

### Act Information
- **Act Title**: Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006 (U.P. Act No. 8 of 2012)
- **Jurisdiction**: State of Uttar Pradesh (Applicable to agricultural holdings, rural lands, and village abadi)
- **Subject Matter**: Settlement of boundary disputes, demarcation of holdings, maintenance of boundary marks (medh), rights of way, removal of obstacles on public paths.

---

### Section 20: Maintenance and Repair of Boundary Marks
1. Every tenure-holder shall be responsible for the maintenance and repair of the permanent boundary marks (medh, stones, or ridges) erected on or demarcating the perimeter of his agricultural holding or plot.
2. In case of joint holdings, the co-tenure-holders shall be jointly and severally liable to maintain the boundary ridges in proper repair according to their recorded shares in the village land records (Khasra and Khatauni).
3. If any boundary mark is damaged, destroyed, ploughed over, or removed, the person bound to maintain it shall restore it within thirty days of notice or direction by the Revenue Inspector or Lekhpal.

---

### Section 21: Duties of Lekhpal Regarding Boundary Marks
1. The Lekhpal of every village shall periodically inspect all boundary marks erected within his halqa (village circle) during the annual field inspection (Girdawari).
2. If the Lekhpal finds that any boundary mark has been injured, destroyed, or removed, or that any agricultural ridge (medh) has been deliberately encroached upon or cut away, he shall record the fact in the village inspection diary (Khasra) and submit an immediate report to the Revenue Inspector and Tehsildar.
3. Upon receiving such report, the Revenue Inspector shall summon both adjacent landholders and attempt an on-spot verification based on the official village cadastre map (Shajra).

---

### Section 22: Penalty for Destruction or Injury to Boundary Marks
1. Any person who wilfully destroys, injures, damages, or removes any boundary mark lawfully erected on agricultural or abadi land, or who cuts down a boundary ridge (medh) so as to encroach upon a neighbor's holding, shall be liable to pay a penalty not exceeding five thousand rupees.
2. In addition to the penalty, the cost of re-erecting, reconstructing, or surveying the boundary mark shall be recovered from the defaulting party as arrears of land revenue.

---

### Section 24: Settlement of Boundary Disputes and Demarcation
1. The Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO) or Assistant Collector may, on an application made by any tenure-holder or upon a report of a revenue officer, demarcate the boundaries of any holding or plot and settle any dispute concerning the same.
2. For the purpose of deciding boundary disputes, the Sub-Divisional Officer shall direct the Revenue Inspector or Lekhpal to conduct an on-the-spot measurement and survey using the official village map (Shajra) and chain measurement (Gunter chain/ETS).
3. The measurement shall verify the fixed reference points (seh-hadda, do-hadda, and fixed village stones) and determine the exact dimensions and area of the disputed parcels as entered in the Khasra and Khatauni records.
4. After completing the measurement, the Revenue Inspector shall submit an inspection report with a field trace map (nazri naksha).
5. If the Sub-Divisional Officer finds that any person has wrongfully encroached upon the land of another by cutting or altering the boundary ridge (medh), he shall pass a summary order directing the encroacher to remove the encroachment and restore the ridge to its lawful position within fifteen days.
6. If the encroacher fails to restore the boundary ridge within the specified time, the Sub-Divisional Officer shall cause the boundary ridge to be restored by the revenue team with police assistance if necessary, and recover the expenses incurred from the encroacher.

---

### Section 25: Rights of Way and Other Private Easements
1. If any tenure-holder or resident of a village is obstructed in the peaceful exercise of any customary right of way, cart-track, cattle path, or water channel (nali) through any agricultural holding, he may apply to the Tehsildar for summary relief.
2. The Tehsildar shall make a local inquiry through the Lekhpal and inspect the village settlement records to verify whether such path or watercourse is recorded in the Shajra or has been customarily used by the villagers for agricultural purposes.
3. If the Tehsildar is satisfied that the obstruction was unlawfully created, he shall direct the person causing the obstruction to remove the same and restore the right of way or watercourse forthwith.

---

### Section 26: Removal of Obstacle on Public Paths and Commons
1. If any person encroaches upon or causes obstruction to any public path, cart-track, threshing-floor (khalihan), pasture land (charagah), or water body (pokhari/talab) vested in the Gram Panchayat, the Sub-Divisional Officer may on his own motion or on complaint order the immediate eviction of the encroacher and demolition of the obstruction.
2. Any person aggrieved by an order under this section may institute a suit in a competent civil court within six months, but the order of the Sub-Divisional Officer shall remain enforceable during the pendency of any informal mediation or revenue conciliation.
