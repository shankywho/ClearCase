# Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 & Code of Civil Procedure (Section 89)
## Statutory Basis for Pre-Litigation Lok Adalat and Non-Binding Community Mediation

### Act Information
- **Act Title**: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (Act No. 39 of 1987) & Code of Civil Procedure, 1908
- **Jurisdiction**: Republic of India
- **Subject Matter**: Pre-litigation dispute conciliation, Lok Adalat compromise awards, alternative dispute resolution mechanisms (ADR) under Section 89 CPC, village legal aid.

---

### Section 19: Organization of Lok Adalats
1. Every State Authority, District Authority, or Taluk Legal Services Committee may organize Lok Adalats at such intervals and places (including rural blocks and panchayat bhavans) as it thinks fit.
2. A Lok Adalat shall consist of serving or retired judicial officers and other persons of repute possessing legal training, social work experience, or standing as village elders/mediators.
3. A Lok Adalat shall have jurisdiction to determine and to arrive at a compromise or settlement between the parties to a dispute in respect of any matter which is falling within the jurisdiction of, and is not brought before, any court for which the Lok Adalat is organized (pre-litigation stage).

---

### Section 20: Cognizance of Cases by Lok Adalat for Amicable Settlement
1. Where in any case referred to Lok Adalat at the pre-litigation stage, the parties mutually agree to refer the dispute, or one of the parties makes an application for referring the matter for amicable settlement, the Authority shall refer the case to the Lok Adalat.
2. The Lok Adalat shall proceed to dispose of each case referred to it and arrive at a compromise or settlement between the parties with utmost dispatch, guided by principles of justice, equity, fair play, and statutory rights.
3. Every Lok Adalat shall act with objectivity and impartiality and endeavor to induce the parties to reach an amicable compromise without coercion or undue influence.

---

### Section 21: Award of Lok Adalat
1. Every award of the Lok Adalat shall be deemed to be a decree of a civil court or, as the case may be, an order of any other court.
2. Where a compromise or settlement is arrived at by a Lok Adalat, no court fee shall be payable, and the award shall be final and binding on all the parties to the dispute, with no appeal lying to any court against the award.
3. In informal or pre-Lok Adalat mediation, the draft settlement serves as a voluntary compromise agreement which the parties may register before the Gram Panchayat or submit to the Taluk Legal Services Committee for formal recording.

---

### Code of Civil Procedure, 1908: Section 89 (Settlement of Disputes Outside the Court)
1. Where it appears to the Court or pre-litigation authority that there exist elements of a settlement which may be acceptable to the parties, the formulation of the terms of a possible settlement shall be given to the parties for their observations.
2. The dispute may be formulated for settlement through:
   - (a) Arbitration;
   - (b) Conciliation;
   - (c) Judicial settlement including settlement through Lok Adalat; or
   - (d) Mediation.
