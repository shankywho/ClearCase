# Indian Easements Act, 1882
## Customary Rights of Way, Cart Tracks, Watercourses and Prescriptive Easements

### Act Information
- **Act Title**: Indian Easements Act, 1882 (Act No. 5 of 1882)
- **Jurisdiction**: Republic of India
- **Subject Matter**: Easements over land, access paths, right of way for farmers and bullock carts, discharge of agricultural runoff and tubewell irrigation water, protection against wrongful obstruction.

---

### Section 4: Easement Defined
1. An easement is a right which the owner or occupier of certain land possesses, as such, for the beneficial enjoyment of that land, to do and continue to do something, or to prevent and continue to prevent something being done, in or upon, or in respect of, certain other land not his own.
2. The land for the beneficial enjoyment of which the right exists is called the dominant heritage, and the owner thereof the dominant owner; the land on which the liability is imposed is called the servient heritage, and the owner thereof the servient owner.
3. Customary village access tracks (pagdandi, rasta), drainage runoffs (pani ka nikas), and seasonal cattle paths are statutory easements recognizing agricultural necessity.

---

### Section 13: Easements of Necessity and Quasi-Easements
1. Where a parcel of land is partitioned among family members or sold out of a larger holding, and the owner of the inner parcel has no means of ingress or egress to a public road or irrigation channel except over the outer parcel, there arises an easement of necessity.
2. The owner of the outer servient plot is legally bound to allow a reasonable right of way or watercourse necessary to cultivate and reach the inner dominant plot, without obstructing passage of farm implements, bullocks, or tractors.

---

### Section 15: Acquisition of Right of Way by Prescription
1. Where the access and use of light or air, or where a right of way or any other easement has been peaceably and openly enjoyed by any person claiming title thereto, as an easement and as of right, without interruption, and for twenty years, the right to such access, way, watercourse, use of water, or other easement shall become absolute.
2. Each of the said periods of twenty years shall be taken to be a period ending within two years next before the institution of the suit or dispute wherein the claim is contested.
3. No temporary or verbal objection without actual cessation of enjoyment shall be deemed an interruption of the prescriptive easement.

---

### Section 33: Remedies and Damages for Disturbance of Easement
1. The owner of any dominant heritage is entitled to maintain a suit or complaint for compensation against any person who causes a substantial disturbance of the easement or has obstructed the customary passage.
2. The disturbance has caused substantial damage when it does a material injury to the economic value of the dominant heritage, or substantially interferes with the cultivation, harvesting, or daily transit of the dominant owner.
3. Informal village Panchayats and mediators may recommend restoration of the original path width and clearing of hedges or barricades within seven days.
